You said you cannot take on more work. Every competitor we looked at has found the one kind of growth that does not need another van.

All six firms we checked across Tampa Bay publish a preventive maintenance agreement or service plan on their own website. It is the first or second thing on most of their homepages.

MJB Services does not mention one anywhere on its site. Not on the homepage, not on the services page, not in the sitemap.

Why This Is The Finding That Matters For You
Because of what you told Paige
  • You are not short of work. You said you do not have the people to take on more. So a lead-generation pitch is the wrong pitch — more calls you cannot answer is a worse business, not a better one.
  • A maintenance agreement is revenue you schedule rather than revenue you chase. The visits go in the calendar months ahead, in the weeks you choose, at a price agreed in advance.
  • It is the only growth line that reduces emergency load instead of adding to it. Every machine on a schedule is a machine less likely to fail at 7pm on a Saturday.
  • It also answers the 20 hours a week. Recurring, pre-agreed work is the easiest kind to systematise — the quote, the schedule and the invoice are the same every time.

What this does and does not mean

A big website is not a big business. 24 Hour Refrigeration's 854 pages tell you they have invested in a website, not that they are winning. We have not seen inside anybody's books, analytics or ad accounts and we make no claim about any competitor's revenue, traffic or results.

What it does mean is that when somebody in Brandon searches for commercial ice machine repair, there are firms with a page built for exactly that phrase, and you have three pages total. That is a structural fact, not a ranking report.

Where You Can Actually Win
You have things they have to buy and you already own
  • Twenty-plus years and a licensed contractor. CAC1813632 is real, checkable, and older than most of the marketing on these sites.
  • All three trades under one licence. AC, commercial refrigeration and ice machines. Most operators keep two or three contractors' numbers on the wall. Being the one number is a genuine offer, and it is what makes a multi-equipment maintenance agreement natural for you and awkward for an AC-only firm.
  • Ice machines are the specialism nobody wants. They are fiddly, they are food-contact, and they are the thing an operator panics about. The Ice Doctors built a whole company on it. You already do the work.
  • Your own photographs. The site has real job photos on it, not stock. That is rarer than it sounds and it is worth more than another paragraph of copy.
  • You do not need more customers to start. A maintenance agreement is sold to people who already know you. That is a call list, not a lead-generation campaign — which matters, because you have said you cannot take more new work.
What We Could Not Check
Marked honestly rather than guessed
  • Actual search rankings. No rank tracker has been run. The page-count comparison is structural evidence, not a ranking report.
  • Google Business Profiles. Not compared for any firm here, yours included. In this trade the map pack often produces more calls than the website does.
  • Contract pricing. No competitor's maintenance agreement price appears anywhere in this document, because we did not obtain any and would not publish one if we had.
  • Their traffic, spend, revenue or results. We have seen public websites and nothing else.
  • Whether you already sell maintenance agreements privately. Entirely possible. The finding is that your website does not mention one — which is a marketing gap either way, and if you do sell them the fix is much faster.
  • Certifications you may hold. Only the state licence number appears on your site. If you hold others, they are invisible.

Six competitors sell a schedule. You sell a call-out.

It is the one growth line that does not need another employee — which is the only kind that is any use to you right now.
